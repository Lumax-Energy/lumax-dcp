1. What this calculator does

This is a screening tool that turns Dynamic Cone Penetrometer (DCP) field readings into an estimated net allowable bearing pressure for a shallow foundation. It does not replace a geotechnical investigation. It is meant for rapid on-site checks, comparing several pad excavations, and planning whether deeper investigation is needed. If Df/B > 2.5 the headline uses the deep method (Skempton / Meyerhof bored pier). A pier adds shaft only when Df/B > 2.5 (deep tip + shaft). At shallower embedment, Hansen depth factors already include some side effect — shaft is shown but not added.

The calculation chain is always the same:

  1. DCP blows → penetration index DPI (mm/blow or in/blow)
  2. DPI → CBR using ASTM D6951 correlation equations (soil-class dependent)
  3. CBR → strength: friction angle φ′ (drained) or undrained strength su (undrained), unless you override them
  4. Strength + footing geometry + groundwater → classical ultimate bearing capacity (Vesić N-factors, Hansen depth factors, shape factors)
  5. Divide by factor of safety → net allowable pressure qa,net
Screening, not design sign-off. DCP–CBR scatter is often about a factor of two. Every step after CBR is an empirical fit. Settlement, consolidation, and site-specific layering still need engineering judgment (and usually more data).

2. Recommended field workflow

  1. Set Project / site once (green save bar under the title).
  2. Excavate to founding level for pad F1.
  3. Drive a DCP from founding level (or ground level — be consistent and note it).
  4. Enter the blow log (or a single average DPI), choose soil model and footing size.
  5. Read qa,net and the Checks panel warnings.
  6. Enter Foundation ID F1 → press Save foundation.
  7. Press New foundation, move to the next excavation, repeat for F2…F10.
  8. At the end of the day export CSV (Excel) or JSON (full backup).

3. Field data (block 01)

Hammer

  • 8 kg (standard) — usual ASTM / SA pavement DCP hammer. DPI is used as measured.
  • 4.6 kg — lighter hammer. The tool halves DPI to an 8 kg-equivalent before the CBR correlation (ASTM practice).

Blow log vs single average

  • Blow log — enter cumulative depth after each set of blows (e.g. every 10 blows). The tool computes layer DPI, weights layers inside the footing’s influence zone, and flags a weak layer if it is much softer than the average.
  • Single average — one DPI applied over the whole influence zone. Use only when the profile is uniform or you only have a summary number.

DCP starts at

Ground (default) — log z = 0 is ground; shaft integrates 0–Df and the tip zone is Df to Df+zi. Founding level — log z = 0 is the underside of the footing; the tip zone is 0–zi in the log and shaft soils were not tested (shaft is not added to a pier headline).

Depth column

Depth is cumulative from the start of the sounding (mm in metric, inches in imperial). Each row’s DPI = (this depth − previous depth) / blows on that row.

Analysis mode

  • Drained — φ′ for granular / long-term strength. Default derivation: φ′ = 25 + 8·log10(CBR), banded 26–42°. This is a published-band fit, not a code equation — override with the ✎ lock when you have better data.
  • Undrained — su for short-term clay capacity. Default: su ≈ 23 · CBR (kPa), roughly the Black/Lister range calibrated mainly on soft-to-firm CBR 1–10. High CBR values will warn that undrained is implausible.

CL/CH selections auto-suggest undrained; general/granular suggests drained. You can still change Analysis manually.

Indicative stiffness E ≈ 17.6 · CBR0.64 MPa (Powell et al. / TRL) is shown for settlement context only — it is not used in the bearing equation.

5. Footing (block 03) — what is width B?

B is the plan width of the foundation you are designing (pad side, strip width, or circle diameter). It is not a property of the DCP cone.

ShapeB meansL
SquareSide lengthSet equal to B
CircularDiameterTreated as B
RectangularShorter sideLonger side (enter L)
StripWidth of long footingVery large (strip factors)

Why B matters

  • Influence zone depth under the footing — about 2B for square/circle up to about 4B for strip. Only DCP data inside this zone (below founding level) govern the weighted DPI.
  • Bearing equation — self-weight term ½·γ·B·Nγ, plus shape and depth factors that depend on B and B/L.
  • Warnings — wide footings (roughly B > 1.2 m) often stress soil deeper than a typical DCP reaches; the tool tells you when the log is too short.

Founding depth Df is the depth of the underside of the footing below ground. Surcharge and depth factors use Df. If Df/B > ~2.5 the tool switches to a deep-foundation method (Skempton undrained or Meyerhof bored-pier drained) for the headline net allowable, and keeps the shallow Vesić/Hansen result as a comparison labelled “not applicable”.

Deep method

Skempton Nc* (clay, cap 9 square/circle and 7.5 strip) and Meyerhof 1976 bored-pier Nq* (sand, no Nγ, limiting qp). Nq* in the table is the bored-pier peak; it is reduced for embedment below the ~10B critical depth (about 40% of peak at the Df/B = 2.5 switch, rising to 100% by 10B). The Nq* table is bored/excavated ≈ 0.4 × typical driven-pile Nq*, not the NAVFAC drilled-shaft row (which is lower) — intentional for this screening tool, then reduced further by the 10B embedment factor. Limiting qp = 50 · Nq*peak · tanφ (kPa), Das/Murthy dense SI. Shaft resistance is integrated along 0–Df when the DCP starts at ground (see Shaft friction). A Pad keeps it off the headline; a Pier / caisson adds shaft only when Df/B > 2.5 (deep tip + shaft). Shallower piers stay on the pad number so Hansen and shaft are not stacked. Still screening, not design sign-off.

Shaft friction

Shaft resistance is a screening estimate integrated from ground (z = 0) to Df using DCP layers (or one DPI in single-average mode, integrating σ′(z) down the shaft). The tip-zone strength is not used for shaft except as a fallback estimate when no layer overlaps 0–Df — and that fallback is never added to a pier headline. If DCP starts at founding, shaft soils were not tested.

  • Pad — headline is end bearing only (existing shallow / deep switch). Shaft is shown as “if this were a pier”, not in the headline. Excavated-and-backfilled pads should stay Pad.
  • Pier / caisson — when Df/B > 2.5, headline qa,net = (Qb,net + Qs) / (A · FS) with Qb,net from the deep tip (never Hansen + shaft stacked). Qs is added only if Df/B > 2.5, DCP starts at ground, and 0–Df has tested cover. A short pier (Df/B ≤ 2.5) stays on the pad number. Use this for a shaft cast against soil.
  • α-method (undrained clay) — α = 0.55 if su ≤ 25 kPa, 0.35 if su ≥ 80 kPa, linear between. fs = α · su.
  • β-method (drained sand, bored) — K0 = 1 − sinφ, δ = 0.75φ, β = K0 tanδ, fs = min(β · σ′, 100 kPa).
  • Layer-wise DCP — each slice overlapping 0–Df converts DPI → CBR → φ or su (4.6 kg hammer halved first). A locked φ or su applies to the tip / end-bearing only, not shaft layers.
  • Critical depth 15B — σ′ is evaluated at min(zmid, 15B), with the same groundwater split as surcharge (dry γ above the water table, γ′ below).
  • Coverage = tested shaft thickness / Df. If the log misses the shaft entirely (or DCP starts at founding), one fallback slice may be shown as an estimate and is not added to the pier headline.

7. Results column

Headline — net allowable bearing pressure

qa,net is the pressure you may apply in excess of the existing overburden at founding level (the usual “net allowable” used for foundation sizing). The subtitle also shows gross allowable and FS. When Df/B > 2.5 the label becomes “Net allowable · deep foundation” and the number comes from the deep method. A Pier / caisson adds shaft to the headline only when Df/B > 2.5 and shaft soils were tested (subtitle “+ shaft”). Shallower piers keep the pad number.

Calculation chain

Shows influence zone, governing DPI, CBR, derived strength, N-factors, the three Terzaghi/Vesić terms (cohesion, surcharge, self-weight), ultimate and allowable values.

Section drawing

  • Footing at Df, spreading influence zone
  • Tested ground vs hatched “no data / extrapolated”
  • CBR bars by layer; weak zone layer highlighted
  • Water table line when set

Checks

Automatic warnings/notes for: incomplete influence coverage, weak hidden layers, DPI outside ~3–60 mm/blow, wide footings, water in the zone, undrained with high CBR, deep-foundation switch (Df/B > 2.5 uses Skempton/Meyerhof for the headline), pier shaft soils not fully tested, settlement-governed granular cases, short-term clay cases.

Print report produces a designed A4 screening sheet (masthead, cloned section drawing, inputs, chain, blow log, checks, and a short “how to read this sheet”) for paper or PDF — not a screenshot of the app.

8. Saving many foundations (F1…F10)

Use the green SAVE · multiple foundations bar under the title, or the Save foundation button under the big pressure result.

  • Project / site — job name shared by all pads
  • Foundation ID — e.g. F1, Pad A, Excavation 3 (required to save)
  • Notes — soil description, RL, weather, refusal, etc.
  • Save foundation — stores full inputs + results in this browser (local storage)
  • New foundation — clears the ID for the next pad (keeps project name and current numbers so you can edit them)
  • Load / × on each row in Saved foundations — reopen or delete
  • CSV — spreadsheet of key results (SI units in the file)
  • JSON — full backup; use Import on another machine or after clearing the browser
  • Clear all saved — wipes browser storage only (not files you already exported)
Data lives in this browser on this device. Export JSON/CSV before clearing site data or switching computers.

9. Limitations (read these)

Not a substitute for a geotechnical report. Do not use this as the sole basis for structural foundation design on critical structures without review by a qualified engineer.
  • DCP was developed largely for pavements; foundation use is an engineering judgment extension.
  • Correlations assume materials similar to the calibration sets — residual soils, laterite, gravel lenses, and fill can mislead.
  • Soundings are shallow; large pads stress deeper ground than the DCP reaches.
  • Bearing capacity is only one limit state — settlement often governs on sand; consolidation on clay.
  • No slope, load inclination, seismic, or layered FE analysis.
  • Undrained su = 23·CBR is for soft–firm remoulded-type ranges; it runs away on stiff/hard materials.

10. Main references behind the numbers

  • ASTM D6951 — DCP test; CBR correlations (USACE-based general / CL / CH equations)
  • Vesić bearing-capacity factors Nc, Nq, Nγ; Hansen depth factors; common shape factors
  • Black & Lister / TRRL-type CBR–su relationship (~20–30·CBR; tool uses 23)
  • Powell et al. (1984) TRL — E = 17.6 · CBR0.64 MPa (indicative stiffness only)
  • φ′ from CBR — band fit for granular soils (override when lab/in-situ strength is known)
  • Skempton (1951) — undrained Nc* for deep footings / piers (cap 9 square/circ, 7.5 strip)
  • Meyerhof (1976) — bored-pier / excavated deep-footing Nq* (no Nγ; limiting qp)
